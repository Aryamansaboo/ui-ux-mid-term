UX Research & Information Architecture — Midterm Submission

Where Did My Complaint Go?

Designing transparency into the hostel maintenance complaint process

Aryaman Saboo · Vijaybhoomi University · August 2026

The Broken Experience

Hostel students lack transparency about what happens to their maintenance complaints after submission — making it difficult for them to know whether the complaint has been received, who is handling it, and what progress has been made toward its resolution.

Phase 1

Empathy & Definition

1.1 — The Problem Space

I picked this problem because I've filed enough hostel complaints myself to know the feeling of typing a message into a group chat and then just... waiting, with no idea if anyone even saw it. Most hostels don't lack a way to file a complaint — there's a register, a WhatsApp group, or a basic form. What's missing is everything that happens after that: an acknowledgment, a status, an owner, a timeline. The complaint goes into a system I can't see into, and the only way to get information back out is to physically go and ask someone. That gap — not the repair itself — is what I'm trying to fix here.

1.2 — Proto-Persona before research

"Rohan" — the assumed frustrated resident

  • Cares mainly about repair speed — the faster the fix, the happier he is
  • Files a complaint and mostly forgets about it until it's fixed
  • Doesn't care who fixes it or when, as long as it eventually happens
  • Complains rarely — treats it as a one-off inconvenience, not a recurring frustration

This was my assumption going in — I sketched this proto-persona from gut instinct before talking to anyone, mostly based on my own experience. Then I tested it against three real interviews, and it turned out to be wrong on almost every point.

1.3 — Talking to Real Residents

I ran three short, semi-structured interviews (15–20 minutes each) with residents from different hostel blocks and years, each of whom had filed a maintenance complaint in the last month. Three people is a small sample, I know — but all three converged on the same complaint almost immediately, which is what convinced me this wasn't a one-off.

1.6 — User Journey Map

Scenario: Ananya's ceiling fan stops working during peak summer. She files a complaint and needs it fixed — and wants to know what's happening while she waits.

1.7 — The Core Pain Point

The lowest point on the emotion line is Day 3 — Follow Up, when a second message gets no clear answer either way. That dip isn't really about the broken fan. It's about the silence.

Between submitting a complaint and it being resolved, hostel residents have zero visibility — no acknowledgment, no status, no named owner, no timeline. That absence of information forces residents into repeated, awkward manual follow-ups, and over time, teaches them not to bother reporting at all.

1.8 — User Stories

As a hostel resident, I want an instant acknowledgment when I submit a complaint, so that I know it actually reached maintenance staff and wasn't lost.

As a hostel resident, I want to see my complaint's current status (Received / Assigned / In Progress / Resolved), so that I don't have to track down the warden in person just to ask.

As a hostel resident, I want to know who is handling my complaint and roughly when, so that I can plan around the disruption instead of guessing.

As a hostel resident, I want to be notified the moment my complaint's status changes, so that I don't have to keep checking manually to find out.

Phase 2

Ideate & Evaluate

2.1 — From Stories to Features

The four user stories above were expanded into a broader list of everything the product might plausibly need — features and content items, not yet organized into any structure.

2.2 — Feature & Content Inventory (20 items)

  1. Submit New Complaint
  2. Select Complaint Category (Electrical / Plumbing / Carpentry / Internet / Cleaning / Other)
  3. Attach Photo / Video Evidence
  4. Auto-fill Room & Block Number
  5. Instant Submission Confirmation
  6. Unique Complaint ID / Tracking Number
  7. Live Status Tracker (Received → Assigned → In Progress → Resolved)
  8. Assigned Technician Name & Contact
  9. Estimated Resolution Time
  10. Push / SMS Notification on Status Change
  11. In-App Comment Thread with Maintenance Staff
  12. My Complaint History (Past & Active)
  13. Escalate a Delayed Complaint
  14. Rate & Give Feedback After Resolution
  15. Mark as Urgent / Priority Flag
  16. Reopen a "Resolved" Complaint
  17. Hostel-wide Maintenance Announcements
  18. FAQ / Self-Help for Common Issues
  19. Complaint Analytics (e.g. average resolution time for my hostel)
  20. "Others Reported This Too" Alert for Similar Nearby Complaints

2.3 — Card Sort

Method: Hybrid card sort — 12 hostel residents, physical index cards for all 20 items above, and four starter categories (Complaints, Track & Status, History, Help) that participants were free to rename, merge, split, or ignore in favor of their own groupings.

Patterns I found

  • Strong agreement (10+/12): Items 1–6 (submit, category, photo, room auto-fill, confirmation, ID) consistently grouped under a "New / Submit a Complaint" category.
  • Strong agreement (11/12): Items 7–11 (status tracker, technician, ETA, notifications, comment thread) were grouped together — and most participants independently created a category literally called "Track" or "Status." Nobody nested these inside a general "history" or "account" bucket.
  • Moderate agreement (8/12): Items 12, 13, 15, 16 (history, escalate, urgent flag, reopen) clustered under "My Complaints," though 4 participants placed "Escalate" with Track instead.
  • Split decision: Item 14 (Rate & Feedback) — 6 participants put it with Track/Resolved, 6 put it with History. No majority pattern.
  • Weak/inconsistent agreement: Items 17, 19, 20 (announcements, analytics, "others reported this too") were placed inconsistently — several participants said these felt unnecessary or unclear, and two invented a new bucket, "Hostel Updates," for them.
  • Strong agreement (10/12): Item 18 (FAQ) consistently grouped under "Help."
Key insight: Residents want Track / Status to be its own first-class, top-level destination — not something buried inside a general history or account section. This directly matches the core pain point from Phase 1: the need for status to be visible, not archived.

2.4 — V1 Information Architecture

Built directly from the card sort patterns above — five top-level categories, with "Track My Complaints" kept as its own first-class destination rather than folded into history.

2.5 — Tree Test

Method: I tested the V1 tree above with 6 hostel residents (none of whom took part in the card sort), giving each a task and asking them to say, out loud, which path they'd follow to complete it. Six is on the smaller side for a tree test, but both failures below showed up almost immediately and repeated across multiple participants, which is usually the sign you've found a real structural problem rather than noise.

Scenario 1

"Your ceiling fan stopped working. You submitted the complaint two days ago. You want to find out who's fixing it and when it'll be done. Where would you look?"

Target destination: Track My Complaints → Live Status Tracker / Assigned Technician

Result: 4 of 6 succeeded directly. 2 of 6 first clicked into My History, expecting their active complaint to live there, before backtracking after ~10–15 seconds.

Scenario 2

"You want to check if anyone else in your block also complained about the water heater. Where would you look?"

Target destination: Hostel Updates → Similar Complaints Nearby

Result: Only 1 of 6 found it there. 3 of 6 looked inside Track My Complaints; 2 of 6 looked inside My History. Nobody thought to check "Hostel Updates" first.

Where users failedWhyHow I fixed the IA
Scenario 1 — 2/6 looked in "My History" instead of "Track My Complaints" Residents don't mentally separate "my active complaint" from "my complaint history" — to them it's one list of complaints in different states, not two destinations Merged Track My Complaints and My History into one top-level "My Complaints" section, split internally into Active / Past tabs
Scenario 2 — 5/6 failed to find "Similar Complaints Nearby" under Hostel Updates Users associate "similar complaints" with the complaint they're already tracking, not with general hostel-wide announcements — it was filed under the wrong mental model entirely Moved Similar Complaints out of Hostel Updates and into the individual complaint's status screen, as related, contextual info

Phase 3

Prioritization

3.1 — MoSCoW: What Actually Ships in v1

Budget is tight, and the brief allows exactly four Must-Haves. Everything is sorted against one test: does this directly close the visibility gap from the core pain point, or is it secondary?

Must-Have 4 / 4

  • Submit a Complaint (category + photo)The front door — nothing else works without it
  • Live Status TrackerDirectly solves the core pain point
  • Instant Confirmation + Unique Complaint IDThe first trust signal — "did it even go through?"
  • Push Notification on Status ChangeCloses the loop without forcing manual checking

Should-Have

  • My Complaints History (Past)
  • In-App Comment Thread with Staff
  • Escalate a Delayed Complaint
  • Assigned Technician Name & Contact

Could-Have

  • Rate & Feedback
  • Reopen a Resolved Complaint
  • Complaint Analytics
  • Hostel-wide Announcements
  • Mark as Urgent / Priority Flag

Won't-Have (v1)

  • Similar Complaints Nearby Alert
  • FAQ / Self-Help
  • Admin / warden dashboard

3.2 — The Hardest Debate

Two features both directly attack the core pain point, and both made a real case for the last Must-Have slot: Push Notification on Status Change and Assigned Technician Name & Contact. I genuinely went back and forth on this one — so instead of picking by gut feeling, I ran them through a DFV Matrix.

3.3 — DFV Matrix

FeatureDesirabilityFeasibilityViabilityTotal
Push Notification on Status Change 5Every interviewee independently said some version of "just tell me, don't make me check" 4Standard push/SMS pattern, well understood, moderate integration effort 4Cuts down admin foot-traffic and repeat questions — real staff-time savings, low ongoing cost 13/15
Assigned Technician Name & Contact 4Valued, but secondary in interviews — "I mostly just want to know it's moving" 3Depends on staff-assignment data being entered accurately every time — fragile at MVP stage 3Raises a privacy question (exposing staff contact info to students) that needs a policy decision first 10/15

Push Notification on Status Change wins, 13 to 10, and takes the fourth Must-Have slot. Assigned Technician stays a strong Should-Have for v1.1, once the staff-assignment data pipeline and the privacy question are sorted out.

3.4 — The MVP

What actually ships in v1: a resident can submit a complaint with a category and photo, get an instant confirmation with a tracking ID, watch a live status tracker move from Received to Resolved, and get a push notification the moment anything changes — without ever having to walk to the warden's office to ask "any update?"

None of this makes repairs happen faster. It just makes the waiting bearable, which — based on everything in Phase 1 — is what was actually broken.
